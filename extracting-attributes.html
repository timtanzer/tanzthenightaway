Extracting attributes

The fastest way to improve your interview performance is to stop preparing for “questions” and start preparing for “themes”. Public hospital interview questions often change wording from panel to panel, but the underlying themes are usually consistent.

This page is a step-by-step method to read a position description and pull out the attributes that are important for the position and likely to be assessed.

First, define what “attributes” means

When people say “key attributes”, they often mean a mix of:

  • Capability (what you can do).
  • Behaviour (how you work with others).
  • Judgement (how you think, prioritise, and manage risk).
  • Values (what you focus on when trade-offs are required).
  • Impact (what changes because you are there).

A common mistake is preparing only for tasks (what you have done) and not preparing to show the panel how you think and behave.

Step 1: Collect your inputs

Read the job ad and role description carefully. Look for any documents that describe the team, the service, or the work area. You can phone the panel chair or speak to someone who works in the area to get more context. The more you understand about the role, the better you can extract the right attributes. Once you get a better idea of common attributes and things that the panel will be looking for, you will get better at asking the right questions when you talk to the hiring manager or team members. Have a think about the role’s scope (self, team, service, system) and how that might influence the attributes they care about, and think about what kind of person would thrive in that role, or what kind of skills will be needed to do the role successfully. Step 4: Group the signals into ~ 5 most important themes

Your goal is to turn the attributes into a small set of themes that you can prepare examples for.

A good target: 5 to 7 themes, plus 1 “bonus theme” if the role is specialised.

Step 6: Prioritise your themes (so you know where to spend time)

Not every theme is equally important. Prioritising helps you practise the highest-value content hardest.

Step 9: “Read yourself” section

After you have extracted themes from the role, the next step is matching those themes to how you actually work. The aim is not to describe your personality in abstract terms. The aim is to pick a few strengths you can demonstrate clearly.
